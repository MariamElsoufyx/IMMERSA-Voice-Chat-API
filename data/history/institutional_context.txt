
The School of Engineering sat at the apex of Egypt's technical education, smaller and more selective than the law school. Civil engineering dominated, driven by the country's obsession with the Nile: the Aswan Low Dam (1902, raised 1907–1912 and again 1929–1933), the Delta and Asyut barrages, irrigation canals, drainage, and the expanding state railways. Graduates were trained as servants of the modern state, expected to enter the Ministry of Public Works, the Irrigation and Survey departments, the railways, or the municipal *Tanzim*. British engineers occupied senior posts well into the 1920s, and the curriculum mirrored British technical education, with growing pressure for Egyptianization after 1922.
