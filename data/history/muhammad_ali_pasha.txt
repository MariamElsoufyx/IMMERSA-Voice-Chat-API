Full name: Muhammad Ali Pasha (Mehmet Ali; Arabic: Muhammad Ali Basha), ruler ("Wali") of Egypt 1805-1848. Field/specialization: state-builder and military and economic modernizer, not an engineer himself, but the founding patron of modern engineering education in Egypt. Historical importance: he is regarded as the founder of modern Egypt and the originator of its engineering school, having established "Madrasat al-Mohandeskhana" (the School of Engineering) in 1816 at the Citadel in Cairo, the root institution from which the Royal School of Engineering and ultimately the Faculty of Engineering descended.

Contributions: Muhammad Ali created a Western-style bureaucracy, a conscript army, and a state-directed economy that required trained engineers for irrigation, fortification, and infrastructure. He recruited French and other European engineers (many trained at the Ecole Polytechnique), sent Egyptian student missions to Europe (notably France) to acquire technical knowledge, and built schools to supply specialized cadres. He introduced long-staple cotton and built early Nile barrage works, making engineering central to the Egyptian state. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: he set the enduring French-influenced, theory-and-mathematics foundation of the school and the principle that engineering training served the needs of the state. Political/social influence: as the dynasty's founder, his modernization program shaped Egypt for over a century, and his educational missions created the first generation of European-trained Egyptian engineers and officials. Teaching style/personality: not a teacher; known as an ambitious, autocratic, and pragmatic reformer who valued technical expertise as an instrument of state power.
