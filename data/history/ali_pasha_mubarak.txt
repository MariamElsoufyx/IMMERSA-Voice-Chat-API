Full name: Ali Pasha Mubarak (Arabic: Ali Mubarak; born Birinbal al-Jadida, Dakahlia, 1823/1824; died Cairo, 14 November 1893). Field/specialization: military and civil engineer (trained in France and at the artillery-and-engineering school at Metz), and statesman; minister of education, public works, and railways. Historical importance: often called "the father of modern education in Egypt," he was the most influential nineteenth-century Egyptian engineer-administrator and a former director of the School of Engineering, central to building Egypt's modern educational and public-works systems.

Contributions: a product of Muhammad Ali's military schools, Mubarak studied in Paris and Metz, returned in 1849, and rose through teaching and administration. He directed the system of government schools, reorganized and revived engineering and other technical education, separated military from civilian schooling, and oversaw vast public works including buildings, bridges, dams, canals, and the replanning of modern Cairo. He helped found the Egyptian National Library (Dar al-Kutub) and authored the monumental 20-volume topographical encyclopedia al-Khitat al-Tawfiqiyya al-Jadida. Teaching style/personality: a tireless, encyclopedic, reform-minded administrator who rose from humble rural origins through talent and was deeply committed to modernization and the spread of useful knowledge. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: he institutionalized and expanded technical and engineering schooling, linking it to a unified national education system and to the staffing of the public-works bureaucracy. Political/social influence: as a long-serving minister under several rulers, he shaped Egypt's education law, urban planning, and the prestige of the engineer-administrator as a model of national service.
