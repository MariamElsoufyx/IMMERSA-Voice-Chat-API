Full name: Ismail Sirri Pasha (Arabic: Isma'il Sirri Basha; born Al-Minya province, 1861; died Cairo, 1937). Field/specialization: civil engineer (Paris-trained, with further training in London) and statesman; repeatedly Minister of Public Works and of Defence. Historical importance: one of the most prominent Egyptian engineer-politicians of the early twentieth century, he served as minister of Public Works and/or Defence in nine governments between 1908 and 1926, making him a central figure in the era's irrigation and infrastructure decision-making that drove demand for engineering graduates.

Contributions: graduating with an engineering diploma from Paris in 1878 and training in London, Sirri embodied the European-educated Egyptian technocrat. As long-serving Minister of Public Works he oversaw the ministry that dominated Egyptian civil engineering, especially irrigation and Nile works, the principal employer of the School of Engineering's graduates. He also translated technical books from English and French and authored work commemorating engineers (Riyad al-Anfus fi Tizkar al-Muhandis). Teaching style/personality: a bilingual, internationally trained engineer-administrator and translator who helped transmit European technical knowledge into Egyptian practice. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: by leading the Ministry of Public Works during the school's key reorganizations (1908, 1916, 1925), he shaped the professional destinations and standards toward which engineering graduates were trained. Political/social influence: his repeated cabinet service placed engineering expertise at the heart of Egyptian governance; his son Hussein Serry Pasha became a notable prime minister, reflecting the family's prominence in the political elite.
