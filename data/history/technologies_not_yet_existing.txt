This entry lists technologies that did NOT exist or were NOT in use in Egypt between 1915 and 1935, to prevent anachronistic errors when describing the period. In water and civil works: the Aswan High Dam (completed 1970) did not exist; only the Old/Low Aswan Dam (1902, heightened to 1933) and the Nile barrages existed. Drip and sprinkler micro-irrigation, large-scale electric pumping stations, and computerized water management did not exist; irrigation was gravity-fed, with steam, diesel, sakia, and shaduf for lifting. Prestressed concrete and tower cranes were not in general use; ready-mix concrete trucks and hydraulic excavators did not exist.

In power and transport: there was no national electricity grid, no hydroelectric power (Aswan hydro came around 1960), and no nuclear power; generation was local and thermal. Mainline diesel and diesel-electric locomotives, mainline railway electrification, and high-speed rail did not exist; mainline traction was steam (burning mazut/fuel oil). In computing and design: there were no electronic computers, no CAD, no BIM, no GIS, no finite-element analysis; all engineering calculation used slide rules, logarithm and trigonometric tables, and graphical methods. In surveying: no GPS or satellite positioning, no electronic distance measurement, no total stations, no laser levels. In communications: no television, no satellites, no microwave or fiber-optic links, no mobile phones, no internet, no FM radio, and no large-scale automatic telephone switching; telephony used manual exchanges, and AM radio broadcasting began only around 1924 (nationalized 1934). In industry: no industrial automation, robotics, CNC machining, or electronic process control, and no significant domestic steelmaking (Helwan steel came in the 1950s). The most advanced widely used technologies of the era were the steam turbine, the diesel engine, reinforced concrete, the electric motor and generator, optical surveying instruments, wired telegraph/telephone, and early AM radio.
