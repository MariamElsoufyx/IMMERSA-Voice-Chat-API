
A student's day began early and ran long. Mornings were lectures — mathematics, mechanics, hydraulics, surveying theory, materials, descriptive geometry — followed by long afternoons in the drawing office, the heart of engineering training. Drafting was relentless: students inked plans, sections, and tracings to exacting standards, often staying late to finish plates due the next morning. Periodic surveying camps took students into the field with chains and instruments to map terrain and practice levelling along canals and roads. Examinations were rigorous, frequently conducted in English, and failure rates were taken seriously; the academic calendar revolved around end-of-year exams that determined progression and ultimately government appointment.
