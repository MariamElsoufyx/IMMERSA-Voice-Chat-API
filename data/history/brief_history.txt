brief History of the Faculty of Engineering at Cairo University

The Faculty of Engineering at Cairo University is the most prestigious engineering faculty in Egypt. Its history dates back to 1816, when Muhammad Ali Pasha ordered the establishment of the Muhandiskhana (School of Engineering), which was originally located at the Citadel of Salah al-Din al-Ayyubi in Cairo. It was relocated several times before settling at its current site in Giza in 1905, and was incorporated into Cairo University as one of its faculties in 1935.

The beginning of engineering education in Egypt traces back to when Muhammad Ali Pasha established the Muhandiskhana at the Citadel in 1816 in the form of preliminary classes. It officially opened its doors as a regular institution in Bulaq in 1834, but soon closed. Subsequently, two schools were established at the Citadel: one for irrigation engineering at the Qanater Khairiya (Delta Barrages) and another for architecture, both founded in 1858, although they too were closed a few years later.

Later, a comprehensive school for irrigation and architecture was opened at Zaafaran Palace in Abbasiya in 1866, and was then transferred to Darb al-Gamamiz. The school consisted of two departments: one for irrigation and another for architecture. The duration of study was five years, including one preparatory year, with specialization taking place during the final two years. The preparatory year was later abolished, reducing the study period to four years, while specialization remained in the last two years.

The specialization system was subsequently abolished, and the school was converted into a single department. The school was also temporarily relocated to the premises of the old School of Agriculture in Giza, pending the completion of its new buildings at the current site in 1905, at which point it was renamed the Royal School of Engineering.

The school was once again divided into two departments for irrigation and architecture, while maintaining the previous system of study. In 1916, the school was reorganized into five departments: Irrigation, Architecture, Municipal Engineering, Mechanics, and Electricity. In 1926, these departments were restructured into four main divisions: Civil Engineering, Architectural Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, and Electrical Engineering. These departments later branched out into various sub-disciplines, accompanied by the establishment and expansion of laboratories and lecture halls.

In 1935, the Royal School of Engineering was annexed to the Egyptian University (now Cairo University) and became known as the Faculty of Engineering. New departments were progressively established within the faculty. The faculty followed a two-semester academic system until it adopted the credit hours system in 2006.
