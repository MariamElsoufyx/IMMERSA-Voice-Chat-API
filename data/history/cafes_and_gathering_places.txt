Cairo's café culture was the social backbone of student life. Neighbourhood *ahwas* (coffeehouses) served Turkish coffee and shisha, with backgammon (*tawla*) and dominoes clattering amid endless argument about politics and football. Downtown (*Wist al-Balad*), more fashionable spots drew the intellectual and effendi crowd; the famous Groppi patisserie and café and the literary Café Riche became known haunts of writers, students, and political talkers. Cafés doubled as reading rooms, debating clubs, and informal study halls — places to share a single newspaper, prepare for exams together, and plan or rehash the latest demonstration.
