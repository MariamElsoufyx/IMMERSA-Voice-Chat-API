
The era predated electronic calculation, so the slide rule was the engineer's essential computing device, alongside printed logarithm and trigonometric tables. The drawing office relied on wooden drawing boards, T-squares, set squares, French curves, brass compass and divider sets, ruling pens, and India ink applied to tracing paper and linen. Surveying depended on the theodolite, dumpy levels, levelling staves, measuring chains and tapes, plane tables, and the planimeter for measuring areas. Students owned and guarded their instrument sets carefully, since a good case of drawing instruments was both expensive and a mark of the profession.
