Full name: Talaat Harb Pasha (Arabic: Tal'at Harb; born Cairo, 1867; died 1941). Field/specialization: economist, banker, and industrialist (not an engineer), and a nationalist economic reformer. Historical importance: as founder of Bank Misr (1920) and its industrial group, Talaat Harb created the demand-side context for Egyptian engineering, building the national industries, above all textiles, that employed engineers and technicians and embodied the economic-nationalist purpose engineering education increasingly served between the world wars.

Contributions: Harb founded Bank Misr in 1920 as "an Egyptian bank for Egyptians," using it to finance a network of national enterprises, including the Misr Spinning and Weaving Company at El-Mahalla El-Kubra (1927), Egypt's largest industrial facility, along with ventures in silk, linen, transport, mining, film (Studio Misr), and aviation (Misr Air, 1932). He notably promoted Arabic as the language of business administration in his enterprises. The textile company even sent technical-school graduates to Europe for training. Teaching style/personality: an energetic, visionary nationalist entrepreneur focused on industrial self-sufficiency and mobilizing local capital. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: his industrial projects created employment and a national-development rationale for producing Egyptian engineers and technicians, and his emphasis on Egyptian ownership reinforced the push to Egyptianize technical expertise. Political/social influence: a towering symbol of Egyptian economic nationalism, Harb's industrialization drive shaped interwar Egypt's economy, labor force, and the prestige of building a domestic industrial and technical base.
