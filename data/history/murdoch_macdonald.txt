Full name: Sir Murdoch MacDonald (Scottish civil engineer; 1866-1957). Field/specialization: civil and irrigation/hydraulic engineering. Historical importance: a leading British irrigation engineer in the Egyptian Public Works service, MacDonald was central to the Nile control works that defined Egyptian civil engineering in the early twentieth century, including the heightening of the Aswan Low Dam and the design of the Sennar Dam for the Gezira scheme, projects that shaped the practical world Egyptian engineering graduates entered.

Contributions: MacDonald worked in the Egyptian Irrigation Service and was involved in major Nile works; he designed the Sennar Dam on the Blue Nile (begun 1914, completed 1925) for the Gezira cotton irrigation scheme, built by British contractors. His career exemplifies the British engineers who dominated Egypt's irrigation administration and whose works were the proving ground for Egyptian-trained engineers. Teaching style/personality: a practical, project-focused senior engineer of the British public-works tradition; not primarily an academic. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: the irrigation and dam projects he led reinforced the irrigation-and-hydraulics emphasis at the heart of the Egyptian engineering curriculum, and Egyptian engineers trained at the Giza school worked under and alongside such British engineers. Political/social influence: the dominance of British engineers like MacDonald in the Irrigation Service was itself a nationalist grievance, since qualified Egyptian engineers were often subordinated to British supervisors, fueling demands to Egyptianize the profession. Note: MacDonald's role is primarily in irrigation engineering practice rather than direct teaching at the school.
