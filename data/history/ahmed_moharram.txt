Full name: Ahmed Moharram Sr. (Arabic: Ahmad Muharram; born 6 December 1913; died 4 March 2017, aged 103). Field/specialization: civil and structural engineering. Historical importance: a distinguished product of the Egyptian University's engineering education in the period, Moharram obtained his BSc in civil engineering in 1936 from Cairo University (then the Egyptian University), just after the Royal School of Engineering became its Faculty of Engineering in 1935, and went on to become one of Egypt's most important structural engineers, consultants, and public officials.

Contributions: he earned an MSc (1946) from the Egyptian University and a PhD in structural engineering from King's College London (1949), then co-founded the engineering consultancy ACE Moharram Bakhoum with Michel Bakhoum in 1950. He taught at Cairo University and later Ain Shams University before public service; as Minister of Housing and Public Utilities in the 1960s he formulated Law No. 6 of 1964, the basis of Egyptian structural design codes, and supervised contracting work on the Aswan High Dam. He proposed and designed Cairo's 6th of October Bridge and Egypt's first cable-stayed bridge. Teaching style/personality: a long-lived, prolific engineer-academic and code-maker who bridged academia, consultancy, and government. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: through teaching, design codes, and a prize named after him at Cairo University, he shaped structural engineering practice and standards for Egyptian graduates. Political/social influence: as a minister and head of the Egyptian Engineering Society for many years, he was a leading figure in national infrastructure and the engineering profession. Note: his ministerial and consultancy career falls after 1950, though his formative education is squarely within the 1816-1950 span.
