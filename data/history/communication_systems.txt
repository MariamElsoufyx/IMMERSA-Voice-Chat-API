Communication systems in Egypt between 1915 and 1935 combined a mature wired telegraph and telephone network with the new arrival of wireless and radio broadcasting. The electric telegraph had operated since the 1850s (the British Eastern Telegraph Company built an early Cairo-Alexandria line), and a telephone line between Cairo and Alexandria was installed in 1881, after which the Egyptian government took over the service and established a Telephone and Telegraph Authority. By 1915-1935, telegraph and telephone lines connected major cities and government offices, and the postal service was well established. Telegraphy used Morse code over wires and submarine cables, while telephony used manual switchboard exchanges operated by human operators.

Wireless (radio) technology arrived and spread during this period. Wireless telegraphy services developed in the early 1920s, and radio broadcasting in Egypt began around 1924 as privately owned and operated stations; in 1934 private broadcasting was abolished and radio was nationalized under a state monopoly (with British Marconi involvement in establishing the official Egyptian State Broadcasting). Communication technology of the era thus included wired telegraph, manual telephone exchanges, wireless telegraphy, and amplitude-modulation (AM) radio using vacuum-tube/valve equipment. Crucially, the technologies that define later communications did NOT exist in this period: there was no television broadcasting in Egypt, no satellite communication, no microwave or fiber-optic transmission, no automatic/digital telephone switching at scale, no mobile telephony, and no internet. Long-distance and international links depended on telegraph cables and early wireless, and frequency-modulation (FM) radio was not yet in service.
