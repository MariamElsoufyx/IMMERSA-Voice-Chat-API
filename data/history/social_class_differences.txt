
Engineering was a prestigious and relatively elite path. The typical student came from the urban middle and upper-middle strata — sons of government officials, landowning *a'yan*, merchants, and established professionals. Coptic Christians were strongly represented in the technical professions. Education here required money, time, and family backing, so the very poor were scarce; a handful entered on merit or scholarship and felt the strain of supporting themselves. Class showed in small things: who arrived by carriage versus on foot, the quality of one's suit, who could afford imported textbooks and instruments, and who frequented expensive cafés versus humble neighbourhood *ahwas*.
