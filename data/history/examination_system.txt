The examination system at the engineering school during 1910-1940 followed the European model of formal year-end and stage examinations governing progression and final qualification. Students advanced through the multi-year program by passing examinations at each stage, with the preparatory and early common years assessed in foundational subjects (mathematics, physics, chemistry, mechanics, drawing) before students could proceed into departmental specialization. Performance was evaluated both in theoretical written examinations and through the practical, laboratory, and drawing components that the curriculum emphasized, consistent with the school's combined scientific-and-practical training mandate.

By-laws issued under the Ministry of Education and Ministry of Public Works (notably the 1886 reforms and subsequent reorganizations in 1908, 1916, and 1925-1926) defined the structure of study and the requirements for completion at each phase. Examination results determined progression between years and ultimately the award of the engineering diploma/degree, and graded appreciation (with classifications such as "Good," "Very Good," and distinction) was used to rank graduates, which in turn affected access to coveted government appointments. Because the great majority of graduates were channeled into Ministry of Public Works and government posts, examinations functioned as a gatekeeping mechanism for entry into the engineering civil service. Note: granular details of exact pass marks, examination formats, and weighting schemes specific to each year of the 1910-1940 Royal School of Engineering are not comprehensively documented in readily available secondary sources; this entry describes the general examination-and-classification framework characteristic of the institution and period.
