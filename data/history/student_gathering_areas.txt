Student gathering areas at the engineering school combined the formal and the informal. Because the campus sat at Giza facing Orman Park and bordering the Giza Zoo, the surrounding gardens and tree-lined avenues gave students green, shaded outdoor space within easy reach, and the area's open, semi-rural character in the early decades of the century made the school feel set among gardens rather than dense city. On campus, students congregated in courtyards, on steps and verandas, under arcades and shade trees between classes, and around the entrances to lecture halls and workshops.

By the 1920s and 1930s, as the institution grew and joined the Egyptian University, student life acquired a more organized social and political dimension: students gathered to debate, to socialize over tea, and increasingly to take part in the nationalist political ferment of the era, since Egyptian university students were deeply caught up in the independence movement and turbulent national politics. Common rooms, refreshment areas or canteens, and prayer space served daily needs. The student body was relatively small and selective, predominantly male for most of the period (the university's first women graduated in 1933, with female participation in engineering remaining limited), creating a close-knit, somewhat elite atmosphere.
