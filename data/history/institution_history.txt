Between 1910 and 1940, Egypt's principal engineering school was the institution descended from "Madrasat al-Mohandeskhana," the School of Engineering founded by Muhammad Ali Pasha in 1816 at the Citadel in Cairo. By the start of this period the school had long been relocated and reorganized: it moved temporarily to the School of Agriculture premises at Giza in 1902, and by 1905 occupied new permanent buildings bordering the Giza Zoo and facing Orman Park, where it remained. During the 1910s and into the 1920s it operated as the "Khedival School of Engineering," and from 1923 it was officially titled the "Royal School of Engineering." In 1935 the Royal School of Engineering was annexed to the Egyptian University (later Cairo University) and renamed the "Faculty of Engineering," a name it has retained ever since. For the entirety of the 1910-1940 period this single Giza institution was effectively the only degree-granting engineering school in Egypt; the Faculty of Engineering at Alexandria (Farouk I) University did not begin teaching until 1942. The term "Al-Mohandeskhana" therefore refers to the historical root and colloquial name of the same institution that became the Royal School of Engineering and then the Faculty of Engineering, rather than to a separate competing school during these decades. The school served as the primary supplier of trained engineers to the Egyptian government ministries, especially the Ministry of Public Works.
