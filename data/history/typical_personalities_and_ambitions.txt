
The archetypal student was ambitious, earnest, and patriotic — a young man who saw engineering as a route to both personal advancement and national service. His dream was a secure, respected post in the Ministry of Public Works, the Irrigation Department, or the railways: to build the dams, canals, and lines that would modernise Egypt and loosen the grip of foreign experts. Many aspired to study abroad on government missions to England or France. Personalities ranged from the diligent, exam-focused careerist to the fiery nationalist firebrand, but most shared a conviction that the modern Egyptian engineer was a builder of the independent nation to come.
