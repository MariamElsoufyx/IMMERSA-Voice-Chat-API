Students were a powerful and visible political force in Egypt between 1910 and 1935, and university and professional-school students, including engineering students at Giza, were repeatedly at the forefront of nationalist agitation. In the 1919 Revolution, students were among the first to act after Saad Zaghlul's exile: students at al-Azhar and then at secondary and professional schools walked out and demonstrated, crowds gathered at railway stations and spread through central Cairo, and student demonstrations helped trigger the strikes by transport workers, lawyers, and civil servants that escalated into a nationwide general strike. Student demonstrations and strikes became near-daily events in March-April 1919.

Throughout the 1920s and into the 1930s, students remained mobilized in support of the Wafd and full independence, and against the British and the palace, frequently clashing with police and facing university closures. A major flashpoint came in the 1935-36 protests: on 13 November 1935 about 2,000 students from Giza (the university, including the engineering faculty) marched toward Cairo, and the following day around 4,000 students clashed with British-led police at the Abbas Bridge, where a student was killed, whose funeral became a mass nationalist demonstration. These protests, demanding restoration of the 1923 constitution, led to repeated closures of the university and contributed to the Wafd's 1936 electoral victory and the 1936 Anglo-Egyptian Treaty. Student political culture mixed nationalist idealism, party loyalties (later including paramilitary youth wings like the Wafd's Blue Shirts and Young Egypt's Green Shirts), and willingness to face real danger.
