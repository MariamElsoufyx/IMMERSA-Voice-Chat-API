The defining marker was the tarbush (red fez), worn by virtually every educated Egyptian man and a badge of the *effendi* class. Students paired it with European-style tailored suits, waistcoats, starched collars, and neckties — the standard dress of the modern Egyptian professional. Appearance signalled respectability and aspiration: a well-pressed suit and a clean tarbush distinguished the engineering student from the *galabiyya*-wearing labourer or fellah. Poorer students made do with a single suit kept carefully brushed. Pocket watches, leather satchels for instruments, and neatly trimmed moustaches completed the look of the ambitious young *muhandis*-in-training.
