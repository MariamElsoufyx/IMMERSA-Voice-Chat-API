Courtyards were a natural and climatically sensible feature of the engineering school's architecture, reflecting both the European institutional quadrangle tradition and the deep Egyptian and Islamic tradition of the central open court. In the hot, dry Cairo climate, courtyards and open inner spaces helped ventilate and shade the surrounding rooms, channeling breezes and providing cooler transitional zones between the strong outdoor sun and the interiors. Buildings were often arranged around or alongside such open spaces, with arcaded or colonnaded walkways giving shaded passage between departments, lecture halls, laboratories, and workshops.

A typical courtyard might be paved or partly planted, with trees, shrubs, or a fountain offering greenery and the cooling sound and sight of water, echoing the garden setting of the wider Giza campus near Orman Park's botanical gardens. Courtyards served as circulation hubs and informal gathering points where students crossed paths, paused in the shade, and assembled before and after classes. Light filtering through arcade arches, the contrast of bright sun and deep shadow, and the relative quiet compared with the clamour of the workshops gave courtyards a distinct calm atmosphere within the working campus.
