The Obelisk on the Cairo University Faculty of Engineering campus is a monument commemorating a two-century-old legacy of Egyptian engineering education. It symbolizes the history of the institution and its role in building modern Egypt.
In 1816, atop the Cairo Citadel, an Egyptian craftsman named Hussein Chalabi Ajwa built a machine that impressed Ruler Mohammad Ali. Mohammad Ali saw it as evidence of Egyptian ingenuity and ordered the creation of a school to teach arithmetic and engineering. This school was called "Mohandeskhana" and is regarded as the origin of the modern Egyptian engineering profession.
By 1834, the engineering school (Mohandeskhana) moved to Boulaq. Its mission was to support the building of the state. Scholars were sent to Europe to study and returned to apply their training in Egypt.
Pioneers and graduates of the engineering school laid the first railway lines across Egypt, designed the barrages that controlled the Nile, and contributed to the engineering of the Suez Canal. These graduates are described as the backbone of Egypt's modernization.
In 1868, under Khedive Ismail, the engineering school was reorganized as the School of Irrigation and Architecture. Graduates such as Ismail Al-Falaki were involved in redesigning Cairo, aiming to model parts of the city after Paris. Much of the iconic downtown Cairo architecture originated from minds trained at this school.
In 1905, the school moved into royal palaces in Giza, the current site of the Cairo University Faculty of Engineering campus. Over time the institution's name changed several times: from Mohandeskhana, to the Royal School, and finally to the Faculty of Engineering, Cairo University.
The Cairo University Faculty of Engineering traces a legacy spanning more than two hundred years. Despite changes in name, rulers, and technology, the institution maintained a standard of engineering excellence. It is credited with producing engineers who shaped modern Egypt's infrastructure and architecture.
Timeline of the institution now known as the Cairo University Faculty of Engineering: 1816 founded as Mohandeskhana at the Cairo Citadel; 1834 moved to Boulaq; 1868 reorganized as the School of Irrigation and Architecture under Khedive Ismail; 1905 relocated to royal palaces in Giza; later named the Royal School, and finally the Faculty of Engineering, Cairo University.
