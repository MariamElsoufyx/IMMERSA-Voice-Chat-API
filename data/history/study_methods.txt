
Study combined rote learning with intensive practical drill. Students memorised theorems, formulae, and definitions for oral and written exams, working from imported English and French textbooks supplemented by carefully copied lecture notes. The drawing office demanded hours of disciplined practice in technical drawing, tracing, and lettering. Surveying was learned by doing — in the field with instruments and field books. Group study was common, students pooling scarce textbooks and quizzing one another before examinations. Logarithm and mathematical tables were constant companions, and proficiency was measured as much by clean, accurate drawings as by recalled theory.
