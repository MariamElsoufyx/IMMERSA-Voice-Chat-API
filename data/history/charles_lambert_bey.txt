Full name: Charles Joseph Lambert, known in Egypt as Lambert Bey (born Valenciennes, France, 2 May 1804; died Paris, 13 February 1864). Field/specialization: mining and civil engineer, graduate of the Ecole Polytechnique (entered 1822, qualified as a mining engineer 1824). Historical importance: a French Saint-Simonian engineer who became Director of the Egyptian School of Engineering from 1838 to 1851, he was a key figure in transplanting the French Ecole Polytechnique model of engineering education to Egypt during Muhammad Ali's reign.

Contributions: Lambert was a disciple of the Saint-Simonian movement (followers of Henri de Saint-Simon, who believed in engineers and industry as drivers of social progress) and came to Egypt in the 1830s, first teaching mathematics in Cairo before leading the engineering school. Under his and other Saint-Simonians' influence, the school's curriculum privileged theoretical study and mathematics on the French model, and Saint-Simonian engineers contributed to major projects including early surveys related to the Suez Canal and the Delta barrage works. Teaching style/personality: associated with the idealistic, technocratic Saint-Simonian belief that scientific and engineering knowledge should reorganize society; an evangelist for applied science. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: he embedded the rigorous, mathematics-heavy French theoretical model that defined the school for decades and trained early Egyptian engineers. Political/social influence: part of the influential French Saint-Simonian presence that linked Egyptian modernization to European industrial ideology, shaping infrastructure planning and the prestige of engineering in the Egyptian state.
