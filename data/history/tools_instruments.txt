The engineering tools and instruments used in teaching during 1910-1940 corresponded to the practical and laboratory-heavy character of the program and to the technologies of the era. Technical and engineering drawing was a foundational skill taught from the preparatory and early years onward, requiring drawing boards, T-squares, set squares, drafting compasses, scales, and related drafting instruments for producing engineering and architectural drawings. Surveying, essential for the dominant irrigation and civil tracks, employed instruments such as theodolites, levels, leveling staves, chains and measuring tapes, and related field-survey equipment for the laying out of canals, roads, and structures.

Laboratory and workshop equipment supported the various departments. Hydraulics laboratories used flow channels, weirs, and apparatus for fluid-mechanics and irrigation experiments. Materials-testing facilities used machines for testing the strength of construction materials such as concrete, metals, and timber. Mechanical workshops contained machine tools (lathes, drilling and shaping machines), steam and thermal apparatus, and machine-shop equipment for hands-on training. Electrical laboratories were equipped with electrical machines, measuring instruments, and apparatus for studying generation, transmission, and measurement of electrical power, reflecting the establishment of dedicated electrical instruction from 1916. Under Charles Andreae's expansion (1928-1937), the school substantially increased its stock of laboratory and workshop equipment. Note: detailed catalogues of specific instrument makes and models from this period are sparse in readily available secondary sources, so descriptions here reflect the standard instrument categories used in early-twentieth-century engineering schools rather than verified itemized inventories.
