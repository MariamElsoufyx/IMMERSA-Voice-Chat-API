Full name: Hassan Afandi, known as "al-Darwish al-Mawsili" (al-Mousely; the Mosul-born dervish). Field/specialization: engineering/mathematics instruction; founding administrator. Historical importance: he was the first head of "Madrasat al-Mohandeskhana," the School of Engineering established by Muhammad Ali Pasha in 1816 at the Citadel (al-Qal'a) district of Cairo, making him the inaugural leader of the institutional lineage that became the Royal School of Engineering and then the Faculty of Engineering.

Contributions: as the first headmaster of Egypt's earliest modern engineering school, he led the institution in its initial Citadel location before it was moved to Bulaq (Boulaq) in 1834. The school's purpose was to train specialized cadres for Muhammad Ali's new military and civil order. Teaching style/personality: little detailed biographical information survives in readily available sources about his personality or pedagogy beyond his role and epithet, which indicates origins associated with Mosul. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: he represents the very origin point of formal engineering instruction in Egypt, the starting node of an institution that has trained Egyptian engineers for over two centuries. Political/social influence: his significance is institutional and symbolic, as the first leader of the school, rather than political. Note: sources on this figure are sparse and primarily limited to the founding records of the school; claims beyond his role as first headmaster, the 1816 founding, the Citadel location, and the 1834 move to Bulaq should be treated with caution given the thin documentation.
