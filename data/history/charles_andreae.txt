Full name: Charles Andreae (born 16 December 1874; died 1964), Swiss civil/structural engineer; sometimes rendered "Charles Andrea" in Egyptian sources. Field/specialization: civil and structural engineering, especially bridge and structural engineering; academic leader. Historical importance: as Director (headmaster/dean) of the Royal School of Engineering at Giza from 1928 to 1937, Andreae led the most important modernization of Egyptian engineering education in the interwar period, reorganizing the curriculum and greatly expanding laboratories and workshops just before the school became Cairo University's Faculty of Engineering in 1935.

Contributions: a distinguished Swiss engineer who had served as Rector of the ETH Zürich (1926-1928) before being granted leave to take up the Egyptian government's offer to lead the Giza school. He was preceded at the school by another Swiss, Louis Potterat. Andreae upgraded the syllabi, lengthened the course of study from four to five years, restored the preparatory year (returning in 1930), and built a large number of new labs and workshops, reorganizing study around the departments of Irrigation, Architecture, Public Works, Mechanical and Electrical Engineering. Teaching style/personality: an experienced academic administrator and structural-engineering authority (later president of the International Association for Bridge and Structural Engineering), he brought continental European rigor and institution-building energy. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: his reforms set the durable five-year, preparatory-plus-specialization structure and the strong laboratory/workshop character of Egyptian engineering training; his tenure also increased the flow of Egyptian students to Swiss institutions such as ETH Zürich. Political/social influence: his appointment reflected King Fuad's policy of reducing British influence by drawing on continental European expertise.
