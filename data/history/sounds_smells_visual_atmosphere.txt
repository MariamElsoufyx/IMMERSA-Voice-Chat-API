The sensory atmosphere of the engineering school campus blended the academic, the industrial, and the garden setting of Giza. Visually, the campus presented pale masonry buildings with tall shuttered windows and arcaded walkways set among trees and open ground, bordered by the greenery of Orman Park and the Giza Zoo, with the strong Egyptian sun casting sharp contrasts of bright light and deep shade across courtyards and facades. Students in suits, tarbooshes (fezzes), and workshop aprons moved between lecture halls, drawing studios, laboratories, and workshops; dust, sun, and the wide Giza sky framed the scene, and in the early decades the surroundings felt semi-rural and gardenlike.

The soundscape shifted from place to place: the relative quiet of lecture halls and the hushed library, punctuated by chalk on blackboards and the scratch of pens; the calm of shaded courtyards; and then the clamour of the workshops, where line-shaft belts slapped and hummed, lathes and machines whirred, hammers rang on metal in the forge, and instructors called over the noise. The hydraulics laboratory carried the sound and smell of running water and damp concrete; electrical labs hummed with machines and smelled of warm insulation and ozone. Smells across the campus included hot metal, cutting oil and grease, coal and oil smoke, sawn timber, chemical reagents, paper and ink, chalk dust, and, drifting from the gardens, dust, foliage, and occasionally animal scents from the neighbouring zoo. Note: this sensory portrait is a reconstruction for worldbuilding, grounded in the documented Giza setting, workshops, and laboratories, rather than a transcription of recorded first-hand accounts of the school.
