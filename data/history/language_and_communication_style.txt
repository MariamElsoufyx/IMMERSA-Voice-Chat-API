
Arabic was the mother tongue and the language of the street, café, and home, but the technical world was multilingual. Under British administration, much instruction and many examinations were conducted in English, the language of engineering and government service, while French retained prestige as the language of high culture and diplomacy. Educated effendis mixed registers fluidly, peppering Arabic conversation with English and French technical terms — *plan*, *section*, *level*, *barrage*. Formal correspondence and reports were stiff and deferential. Among themselves, students spoke colloquial Egyptian Arabic laced with political slogans, professional jargon, and the slang of their generation.
