Full name: Mahmoud Fehmy (Arabic: Mahmud Fahmi; born c. 1842/1843; died 1890). Field/specialization: military and civil engineer; chief engineer of the Egyptian Army and Minister of Public Works. Historical importance: a prominent Egyptian engineer of the late nineteenth century and a nationalist figure of the Urabi movement (1879-1882), he represents the early generation of Egyptian-trained military engineers who linked engineering competence to national and political causes.

Contributions: as chief military engineer under the leader Ahmed Urabi, Fehmy was responsible for designing and constructing Egypt's military fortifications during the 1882 Anglo-Egyptian War, including the defensive works engaged at the battles of Kafr el-Dawwar and Tell el-Kebir. He later held the post of Minister of Public Works. Teaching style/personality: known as a capable, patriotic military engineer; not primarily an academic teacher. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: he exemplified the military-engineering tradition out of which the School of Engineering grew and demonstrated the application of fortification and field engineering by Egyptian nationals. Political/social influence: his participation in the Urabi revolt made him a symbol of Egyptian nationalist resistance to foreign control; the failure of the revolt and the subsequent British occupation (from 1882) reshaped the context in which engineering education would develop, shifting it from French toward British influence. Note: detailed records of Fehmy's specific teaching or curricular role are limited; his significance lies chiefly in military engineering and nationalist politics.
