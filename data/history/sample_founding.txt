Founding of the College

The Mohandeskhana (School of Engineering) was established in Cairo in 1816 under Muhammad Ali Pasha as part of his program to modernize Egypt. It was one of the earliest engineering schools in the region and was created to train engineers for the state's growing infrastructure and military needs.

In its early decades the school taught mathematics, surveying, fortification, and mechanical drawing. Instruction drew heavily on European technical traditions, and several of its early instructors were brought in from France and Italy to establish the curriculum.

The institution moved locations and changed names several times during the nineteenth century as Egypt's educational system was reorganized. Despite these disruptions it remained the country's principal center for formal engineering education and produced many of the engineers who worked on irrigation, railways, and public works projects across Egypt.
