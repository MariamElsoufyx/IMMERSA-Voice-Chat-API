Railways in Egypt between 1915 and 1935 were extensive, mature, and steam-powered, forming one of the oldest and densest networks in Africa and the Middle East. The first line, Alexandria to Cairo, opened in 1854-1856 (the first railway on the African continent), and by the 1920s the standard-gauge (1,435 mm) Egyptian State Railways operated roughly 2,250 route miles, running along the Nile Valley and across the Delta, with connections to Suez and Upper Egypt. Alongside the state network were narrow-gauge lines, including the privately operated Egyptian Delta Light Railways (750 mm gauge) serving the agricultural Delta, sugar-estate lines in Upper Egypt, and the Western Oases line to Kharga. The Luxor-to-Aswan line was converted from narrow to standard gauge during the 1920s.

Motive power was the steam locomotive, typically fueled by mazut (fuel oil) rather than coal, which Egypt lacked. The Egyptian State Railways purchased large numbers of locomotives from British builders (such as North British Locomotive Company and Armstrong Whitworth) and also German builders (Borsig), with classes like the 2-6-0 "545" class delivered from 1928 and 1931. Technology of the era included steam traction, mechanical and vacuum/air braking, semaphore signaling, telegraph-based train control, and water and fuel-oil servicing facilities. Importantly, mainline diesel and diesel-electric locomotives, widespread mainline electrification, and high-speed rail did NOT yet exist in Egypt during 1915-1935; the only electrified passenger rail was light/suburban and tram service (for example the Cairo-Helwan electric line and the urban electric tramways), not the main steam network.
