Full name: Evelyn Baring, 1st Earl of Cromer ("Lord Cromer"; born 1841; died 1917). Field/specialization: British colonial administrator and financier (not an engineer); British Consul-General and effective ruler of Egypt from 1883 to 1907. Historical importance: Cromer's policies fundamentally reshaped Egyptian engineering education by ending direct French dominance and steering the School of Irrigation and Architecture (later the Royal School of Engineering) toward a British, practice-oriented model serving Britain's economic interest in Egyptian cotton.

Contributions (in the context of engineering education): under Cromer's administration, new by-laws were established for the School of Irrigation and Architecture in 1886 that imposed tuition fees, limiting access, and replaced French instructors with British ones who emphasized practical training over French-style theory. British irrigation engineers were brought in to supervise the ministries and the school, prioritizing the maximization of cotton output. Teaching style/personality: Cromer was an austere, autocratic, cost-cutting administrator often criticized for "regressive" educational policies that constrained higher education spending. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: he initiated the long-term shift from the French theoretical model to the British practice-and-workshop model, and the emphasis on producing engineers for immediate government service. Political/social influence: as the architect of Britain's "veiled protectorate," Cromer's prioritizing of fiscal control and cotton exports over Egyptian industrial development became a grievance fueling Egyptian nationalism, which in turn later drove demands to Egyptianize and modernize engineering education.
