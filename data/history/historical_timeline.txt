Modern engineering education in Egypt began in 1816 when Mohammed Ali Pasha established Madrasat El-Mohandeskhana (School of Engineering). The institution was created to prepare skilled technical specialists for the state, particularly for military and infrastructure development. It formed part of a broader national educational system that included primary, secondary, technical schools, and overseas scholarship missions during the 19th century.

During the late 19th century, Egyptian higher technical education underwent several periods of restructuring and decline under the successors of Mohammed Ali Pasha. Following the British occupation of Egypt, educational reforms under Lord Cromer focused on limited and utilitarian education designed primarily to serve colonial administrative and governmental needs rather than broader intellectual or scientific advancement.

A national movement advocating for a modern and secular university eventually led to the establishment of the Egyptian University in Cairo in 1908. The university was envisioned as a liberal academic institution centered on knowledge and scientific progress. In its early years, many faculty members were European professors, while Egyptian scholars were sent abroad to receive advanced education and prepare for future academic positions.

The Egyptian University, initially founded as a private institution, was reorganized as a state university in 1925 under King Fuad I and later became known as Fuad I University. It gradually developed into Egypt’s leading national university and played a central role in shaping modern higher education and scientific research in the country.

Within this university framework, engineering education was formally institutionalized through the School of Engineering. Electrical engineering courses were introduced in 1916, marking one of the earliest organized electrical engineering programs in Egypt. In 1927, Mechanical and Electrical Engineering were combined into a single department to support expanding industrial and technological education.

In 1932, King Fuad I inaugurated a dedicated electrical laboratories building for the School of Engineering. The development of specialized laboratories reflected the growing importance of practical engineering education and scientific experimentation during Egypt’s interwar modernization period.

In 1935, the School of Engineering was officially renamed the Faculty of Engineering, a title it continues to hold today. The faculty consolidated multiple engineering departments and expanded during a period marked by Egyptian nationalism, academic Egyptianization, and increasing competition between local and foreign academic influences under continued British political dominance.
