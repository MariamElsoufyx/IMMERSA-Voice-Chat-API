The departmental structure of the engineering school changed several times between 1910 and 1940. In October 1908 the study was divided into two departments, Irrigation and Architecture, with specialization in the final two years. In 1916 a law reorganized the school to offer specialized studies in the final two years across five departments: Irrigation, Architecture, Municipal (Public Works), Mechanical, and Electrical Engineering. In 1925-1926 the school underwent a major reorganization and these specializations were consolidated into three principal departments: Civil (combining irrigation, public works, and municipal subjects), Architecture, and Mechanical-Electrical (Power) engineering.

The subjects taught reflected this structure. The Irrigation/Civil track emphasized hydraulics, irrigation engineering and structures, water supply, surveying, soil and earthworks, bridges, roads, and reinforced concrete, reflecting Egypt's heavy dependence on Nile irrigation infrastructure. The Architecture department covered building construction, design and drawing, materials of construction, and municipal/sanitary works. The Mechanical department taught machine design, steam and thermal engineering, mechanics, and machine drawing. The Electrical department taught electrical machines, electrical measurements, and power generation and transmission. Across all departments, the early "preparatory" and common years carried heavy mathematics, mechanics, physics, chemistry, and technical drawing. The strong weighting toward irrigation and public works subjects mirrored the government's priorities, since most graduates entered the Ministry of Public Works and related irrigation and infrastructure services.
