Full name: Ahmed Lutfi el-Sayed (Arabic: Ahmad Lutfi al-Sayyid Pasha; born Berqin, Dakahlia, 15 January 1872; died 5 March 1963). Field/specialization: lawyer, journalist, philosopher, and nationalist intellectual; university administrator (not an engineer). Historical importance: as the first rector/president of the Egyptian University (later Cairo University), serving from 1925 to 1941, he led the institution during the very years when the Royal School of Engineering was incorporated as its Faculty of Engineering in 1935, and he was a defining figure in shaping the modern Egyptian university.

Contributions: known as the "Professor of the Generation" and "teacher of an entire generation," Lutfi el-Sayed was a leading liberal and secular nationalist who co-founded the Umma Party, edited the newspaper al-Jarida, and translated Aristotle into Arabic. As rector he fought for university autonomy from palace and government interference and oversaw the period when the first women graduated and when engineering joined the university. Teaching style/personality: a principled, scholarly liberal; he resigned in protest in 1932 when his colleague Taha Hussein was removed for political reasons, demonstrating his defense of academic freedom. Influence on Egyptian engineering education: by integrating the engineering school into the university and defending institutional autonomy and the Egyptianization of higher education, he helped transform engineering training from a ministry-run technical school into a university faculty. Political/social influence: a central architect of Egyptian liberalism, secularism, and "Egypt for the Egyptians" nationalism, he influenced generations of the political and professional elite, including engineers educated under the university he led.
