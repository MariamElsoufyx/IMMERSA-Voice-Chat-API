
Most students reached the school by Cairo's electric trams, introduced in 1896 and by this era a dense, cheap network threading the city — the everyday transport of the effendi class. Egyptian State Railways carried provincial students to the capital and home on holidays. Within neighbourhoods, students walked, rode bicycles, or hired a horse-drawn *hantur* (carriage) when funds allowed. Donkeys still served on the city fringes and in rural surveying assignments. Private automobiles were rare luxuries of the wealthy elite, becoming slightly more visible among the privileged by the late 1920s and 1930s, but ordinary students relied on trams and their own feet.
